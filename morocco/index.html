Economic Warfare

Advertising as the Primary Enforcement Mechanism

1
Coordinated boycotts — ONA/SNI (Al Mada predecessor) withdraws ads from critical outlets, signaling state enterprises and allied businesses to follow. Nichane, TelQuel, Le Journal → all closed.
2
Propping up compliant outlets — Revenue of pro-government newspapers is 11–14× their sales revenue. Massive advertising subsidies unrelated to readership.
3
Opaque state ad distribution — No transparent criteria for who gets state advertising. MOM calls it "an opaque and unjustified procedure."

"The flip side of the boycott involves encouraging wealthy businessmen close to the government to create well-funded media groups that pour millions of dirhams into officially-sanctioned advertising in newspapers that might have a circulation of no more than 2,000."
— Carnegie Endowment, 2010

The Limits of Journalism

The Three Red Lines

A mirror of the national motto: Allah, al Malik, al Watan

✕ The Monarchy ▶
Penal Code Art. 267.5 — 1 to 3 years' imprisonment
The royal family's business interests and personal lives cannot be critically examined. The 2016 Press Code eliminated prison sentences for press offenses — but parliament simultaneously inserted Art. 267.5 into the penal code. Journalists are now tried as ordinary criminals.
✕ Western Sahara ▶
Territorial integrity laws — undermining national unity
Western Sahara must be discussed exclusively as an integral part of Morocco. Any reference to "occupation," self-determination, or supporting Sahrawi rights is prosecutable. Ali Anouzla faced 30 years under the anti-terrorism law for linking to a critical article.
✕ Islam ▶
Anti-terrorism Law 03-03 — 2 to 6 years
Islam cannot be criticized or questioned. The anti-terrorism law with Article 218-2 punishes "glorifying" terrorism with 2–6 years and allows 12-day pre-arraignment detention without charge. Used against journalists covering extremism critically.
~80% of Moroccan media professionals admit to practicing self-censorship. — CPJ

Three Movements That Shocked the Palace

When algorithms replaced editors

2011
February 20 Movement — Arab Spring-inspired. Facebook groups organized simultaneous protests in 50+ towns (expanding to 110 by April). Facebook was searched more than Google in Morocco that year. Citizen outlet Mamfakinch became the first citizen journalism platform.
2016
Hirak Rif Movement — Triggered by fishmonger Mouhcine Fikri crushed in a garbage truck. Nasser Zefzafi used Facebook Live: one broadcast garnered 350,000 followers, 13,000 shares, 33,000 comments in under 20 minutes. 700+ protest events. Government response: 150+ arrested, sentences up to 20 years.
2018
Boycott Movement — Anonymous activists targeted Afriquia Gas, Sidi Ali water, Centrale Danone via WhatsApp and Facebook. Within 2 weeks: 90%+ awareness, 54% participation. Entirely leaderless — impossible to arrest organizers.
