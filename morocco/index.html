The three red lines

Cross them and you will not be spared. 80% of Moroccan media professionals admit to self-censorship.

Monarchy
The royal family's business interests and personal lives cannot be critically examined.
Territory
Western Sahara must be discussed exclusively as an integral part of Morocco. Any reference to "occupation" is prosecutable.
Islam
Cannot be criticized or questioned.
Article 267-5 of the penal code: "causing harm" to Islam or the monarchy → 1 to 3 years imprisonment.
The 2016 press code "abolished" prison for journalists — but parliament inserted the charges into the penal code the same day.

The weaponized charge

"False allegations of sexual assaults and extramarital relations have been abusively and routinely used over the past years by Moroccan authorities as a tool to silence and jail critical journalists."

— the paradox —

The palace can imprison
a journalist.

It cannot imprison an algorithm.

Decades of control — SNRT, Al Mada, HACA, advertising, the red lines — worked when information flowed through owned channels. Now 78% of Moroccans get news from Menlo Park and Mountain View.

The dual response

Externally — Digital Morocco 2030

• Top-50 global e-gov ranking
• 3,000 startups
• 500 MW Dakhla data center
• Oracle cloud in Casablanca & Settat
• Mistral AI for Arabic & Amazigh
• "Cloud First" national roadmap

Internally — extending the old playbook

• HACA expansion into a "digital watchdog"
• Content removal orders to global platforms
• June 2023 — elected press council dissolved
• €100,000 deposit required for new outlets
• Joint DGSN/DGST/DGED warning to Moroccans abroad

Researchers call it "infrastructural capture" — Moroccan newsrooms depend on Google and Facebook for tools, audience analytics, and funding. The state controls traditional media; global platforms control the digital infrastructure both rely on.
